I watched both clips at actual 124 CSS on light and dark, then inspected the enlarged neckline sheet. The attention pose preserves the Sparrow's silhouette, plumage and lighting; lower body, wing/tail and feet remain quiet. The small contour redistribution is acceptable, with a mildly abrupt enlarged upper-neck cluster transition still visible. Fixed-body equality alone did not determine this pass. Old D's earlier provisional usefulness remains unchanged; this is an optional refinement, not a demonstrated premium-quality breakthrough.

The roll states are actual nearest-neighbor transformations of visible source head pixels about a documentary pivot. At normal size the tilt is slight and the neck/jaw edge can flicker. Enlargement exposes a broken horizontal throat/cutout at −6° and notched or disconnected shoulder-neck clusters at +6°. The retained 14 uncovered cutout pixels per nonzero angle corroborate the visible seam; the count is not an automatic rejection rule. No hidden neck artwork was invented. These controls are 2D in-plane roll, not 3D yaw or a calibrated biological joint.

Own live playback verified the authored 30 Hz clocks: attention 60/18/12, with neutral return at tick 78; roll 45/12/6/12/15, returning at ticks 57 and 75. Both end at tick 89 and hold through 3000 ms with no page errors. Images were examined in RAM; no capture files were saved.

All 62 frozen new inputs and 47 frozen old D inputs remain exact. This reviewer generated, exported or edited no pixels. Existing authored head drawings were reused; refinement and roll IDs are derivatives, adding no native drawings or semantic slots. Retain the bounded attention refinement with its caveat. Hold the nonzero roll controls until real compatible neckline/occlusion art is available; no additional render trials or hidden filling were performed here.
