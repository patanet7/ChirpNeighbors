## Biological evidence and its limits

* Ingersoll & Lentink's [Anna-specific hover study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/221/20/jeb178228/19706/How-the-hummingbird-wingbeat-is-tuned-for) filmed six males at a stationary feeder. Weight support occurs during both strokes and is asymmetric. Stroke reversal is mechanically important. These findings support drawing both return and power strokes; they do not prescribe a game frame count or a universal wingbeat rate.
* Sapir & Dudley's [Anna-specific backward-flight study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/20/3603/11039/Backward-flight-in-hummingbirds-employs-unique) measured hovering, forward and backward flight in a wind tunnel. Reverse flight involved a steeper body, head flexion and altered stroke-plane orientation. Its description of approach, hover-feeding and retreat supports that action order. Use a distinct reverse posture; do not reverse a forward root path and claim matching body mechanics. Laboratory sustained backward flight does not measure every brief garden retreat or landing.
* The [take-off study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/207/8/1345/15063/Take-off-mechanics-in-hummingbirds-Trochilidae) concerns **rufous hummingbirds**, not Anna's. Wings were active during leg thrust. This is comparative support for wings-before-release, not Anna-specific transition timing. No numerical leg-force allocation is transferred into the plan.
* Cornell's [official identification](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Annas_Hummingbird/id) supports a stocky green/gray individual with a straight, relatively short bill, rose male crown/gorget, and a tail beyond the folded wingtips. It lists approximately 10 cm total length and 12 cm wingspan. Head angle and illumination change gorget brightness. Keep a readable rose patch in the selected view; do not animate random whole-body color flashes. Cornell's [life history](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Annas_Hummingbird/lifehistory) supports flower-to-flower flight and regular perching between bouts. No abundance claim is needed.

The published hover/hum work by [Hightower et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/63107) is a relevant additional primary source, but its full publisher page and PMC mirror returned challenge pages here. Its abstract is accessible through the [authors' preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01933). It is not treated as a verified reusable-media license in this packet.
