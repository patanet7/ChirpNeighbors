## Common art direction and output requirements

Detailed, recognizable species art; simpler and quieter scenery/objects. Consistent right-facing side profile, slight top-plane visibility for objects, fixed top-left daylight in source art. Avoid an isometric camera, fantasy mascot proportions or equally noisy birds and backgrounds. Let the existing environment lighting provide day/dusk/night shifts; do not generate unrelated palette-shifted species identities.

Candidate starting scene swatches are current `fieldnotes` colors: sky `#dce9db`, grass `#779267`, foliage `#446b51`, wood `#d1bc94`, stone `#e4dabb`, water `#8bbebb`. These are continuity references, not a newly approved palette. Choose and lock bird palette separately; bird identity colors must not be blindly theme-recolored. Keep a machine-readable palette and per-asset color count; private V3/V4 normalization allows at most 64 palette colors.

Every deliverable retains creator, source bytes/hash, source role, license and attribution, intended reuse, exact generation prompt and tool identity where applicable, repair/crop/registration history, reviewer and immutable version. Do not invent an underlying model name or seed that the tool did not supply. Source rights, output terms, species fidelity and technical validity are separate gates. Generated concepts are not biological reference replacements.

## Production brief A: one European robin

**Identity:** `cn-species-erithacus-rubecula`, European Robin, _Erithacus rubecula_. Global canonical species art first; personal naming does not recognize an individual wild bird. Variants come after the canonical release. Reference review must check actual admitted side-view bytes: beak proportion, eye position, orange face/breast boundary, olive-brown upperparts, pale belly, leg/toe count and complete tail. Board labels or identifier validation cannot establish these features.

**Master-pose brief:** one complete right-facing standing robin with quiet head posture, consistent body scale and visible support feet, plus the same design in a bath-contact pose. Preserve a complete tail and thin toes. Choose a column's cluster language; do not mix its body with a differently proportioned head. No scenery, UI, shadows baked into surrounding transparency, random texture or species-inaccurate decoration. A generated master must be reviewed and registered before further poses are requested.

### Density and world size are separate decisions

The existing generic fixture has a padded 75×60 world canvas, pivot `(36,48)` and five unique frames. Its opaque silhouette is smaller than its canvas. That padded envelope is not a biological scale standard. Legacy illustrative robin scaling is 0.95; V4 density-aware art uses explicit source-to-world scaling and reviewed shape geometry instead of silently inheriting that tuning.

Current private V3/V4 limits: source edge ≤256; ≤64 unique frames; decoded `width × height × uniqueFrames ≤65,536`; ≤4,096 rectangles per frame and ≤16,384 rectangles total. V4 density is exactly 1, 2 or 4 source pixels per world unit. Densification does not increase world size or bypass budgets. Examples below are **candidate specifications for review**, not instructions to resize the concepts:

| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Existing padded size at 2×      | 150×120 / 2 / 75×60               | 3                                | Cannot hold even five unique frames.                                                                 |
| Existing padded size at 4×      | 300×240 / 4 / 75×60               | 0                                | Source edge and aggregate budget reject it.                                                          |

| Larger detailed test candidate  | 128×96 / 2 / 64×48                | 5                                | Five frames use 61,440 decoded pixels; limited gesture diversity must be disclosed.                  |

Choose world silhouette and footprint in a 960×540 yard with the actual 100-world-unit-wide bath before selecting the source canvas. If the accepted detail and motion need more frames than fit, design a versioned bounded resource-contract change and migrate readers; do not silently raise limits, shrink the torso between poses or call upsampled fixture pixels new detail. The older 32×32/60-frame recipe is mechanically possible at its proposed source size but remains unapproved for this art target.

**Contact/registration contract:** the world support point remains the occupied scene slot regardless of local canvas pivot. Use V3/V4 per-pose integer source `pivot`, explicit `left-foot`/`right-foot`/`perch`/`ground` contacts as applicable, shape `support`, visual bounds, landmarks and conservative footprint. Source annotations scale by `1/density` into world geometry. Do not add an invented `beak` or `water` contact enum: beak/waterline interaction is a separate visual review until a contract extension exists. Retain exact pose IDs and source/manifest pins.

**First motion test candidate:** two idle poses, two moving/hop poses and four bathing poses, eight unique source frames in the compact test candidate. This is a bounded test scope, not a complete nine-action release. Perch may deliberately reuse idle with that fact disclosed. Singing, feeding, sleeping, playing and nesting still need actual authored coverage or disclosed existing fallback; do not claim a missing clip is complete. A later polished pack can use a reviewed different canvas/frame plan or evolved bounded contract.

Use existing 30 Hz clip timing; choose duration ticks explicitly, not GIF frame guesses. Review body-width stability, complete appendages, consistent foot baseline, intentional hop displacement, loop closure and entry/exit to idle. Current engine has one right-facing mirrorable direction, `moving` shared across movement contexts, and no new takeoff/landing controller or cross-clip blend just because art exists. Compare each clip at source scale and at actual world scale in the existing Pixi engine, with SVG and reduced-motion fallback.

**Acceptance:** admitted rights/reference bytes; named species-fidelity approval; approved direction/palette/world size; binary alpha and clean hidden RGB; logical grid/palette/frame/rectangle budgets; verified pose/shape support; no clipped anatomy or unintentional scale drift; reviewed mirror; actual bathing reservation/contact/depth video plus static contact sheet; exact immutable pins and rollback. Current shared catalog rendering projects V2 only: private V4 quality acceptance does not by itself enable global density-aware publication.

## Production brief B: one layered rural garden

**Direction choices:** use the rural half of the yard board as the composition input; courtyard remains a distinct later regional pack. Keep the distant wall/shed and open lower lawn; reduce foreground flowers, leaf texture and strong ground shadows that compete with a robin only tens of world units wide. Do not bake the bath, feeder, perch, birds or weather particles into the scenery.

**World/staging:** preserve 960×540 world coordinates. Current garden placement zone is x=40…920, y=340…510; roaming area x=70…890, y=375…507; existing authored ground slots/flight corridors remain authoritative. Start with a horizon around y=300 only as a staging proposal, then validate scene silhouettes against those zones. Do not infer metric terrain slopes from the concept painting.

**Separate outputs:** (1) sky/remote background; (2) quiet ground; (3) restrained foreground mask. Supply editable source and lossless versions with matching canvas origin, palette/style version, explicit opaque/transparent role and separate masks. Source density for backgrounds is a distinct decision from bird density. Current runtime scene V1 is technical inline palette geometry; a production bitmap-layer contract with bounded decoding/admission is a prerequisite. A 960×540 concept is not a valid bird manifest and must not be routed through the bird pixel budget. Production scene resource ceilings must be specified/reviewed rather than assumed from a screenshot.

**Depth/lighting:** background order 0, ground 1, objects/birds share ground-Y depth order 2, foreground 3, weather/light 4. Actors add 0.25 to support depth under the current contract. An object's apparent height must not replace its ground depth. Review foreground occlusion at actual bird positions; keep playable objects and the bath access points readable at dawn/noon/dusk/night. Source highlights should remain calm enough for continuous existing environmental tinting.

**Acceptance:** layer alignment/origin, no embedded interactive objects, preserved placement/walkable/flight geometry, reviewed foreground masks, no edge seams, dominant bird readability, palette/night readability, reduced motion, bounded scene admission and representation, exact source/license/manifest versions. Photo-derived personal yards remain a planned private workflow, not a shortcut around geometry or rights review.

## Production brief C: one birdbath and water surface

**Direction options:** A limestone / B sage-gray stone / C terracotta-cream from the actual new comparison board. Select the same material family for the body and foreground rim. Reduce the generated sparkle/highlight density; water should communicate a quiet surface, not compete with the robin. The lower ellipses are separation studies, not a working liquid solver or registered atlas.

**Existing physical catalog contract:** kind `bath`; ground footprint width 100, depth 54; height 80 world units; ground origin `(x,y)` is the object's placement. Existing left/right bath supports are `(x−18,y−68)` and `(x+18,y−68)`. Example existing fixture placement `(700,445)` gives support points `(682,377)` and `(718,377)`. Preserve these offsets for an art-only replacement. Catalog changes require coordinated domain, collision, placement and behavior review; concept silhouette does not grant new slots/capabilities.

**Layered art proposal:** fixed base/pedestal and rear rim; independently masked water surface; foreground lip that can occlude the correct part of the bird without hiding its face. Author local extents and ground pivot explicitly, fit full object height/footprint, mark the two foot seats and provide reviewed water/occlusion masks. Current catalog fixture drawing uses a water strip near y=−71, but that is a visual fixture, not an authoritative bowl elevation. Review feet at the actual −68 support and avoid feet floating above the rim or clipping through it. The current object renderer is primitives: separate bitmap rim/water layers need an explicit representation/admission contract before production integration.

**Source-size proposal, not approval:** use local coordinates and density chosen for the 100×80 object visual envelope; e.g. 2× gives 200×160 source artwork before any separately justified padding. Keep object and water masks registered to that local origin. Do not copy bird source-budget numbers onto objects: define a reviewed object/water texture and total scene-allocation budget. Keep dynamic water geometry under existing bounded prototype limits while evaluating art; a new resource contract must preserve actor admission headroom.

**Water motion brief:** calm base surface; event-driven concentric ripple response; a restrained splash contact response; separate optional precipitation impacts. Request/author isolated effects with explicit origin, lifetime and maximum extent so they remain inside the reviewed water mask. Prefer reusable effects over animating/changing the whole stone bath. Review effect phases on both facing birds, during seat contention, movement onto/off the bath and interruption; no per-frame unbounded texture creation. Do not bake the robin into the water sheet.

The running prototype uses dimensionless cell-depth sums, pixel snow and normalized visual melt. It is not calibrated hydrology. Drinking, per-bath physical storage and dry-bath behavior gating are not implemented. Art must not imply those mechanics are available. The [surface-weather architecture](../../architecture/surface-weather-model.md) separates reviewed pure-core work from remaining renderer, object topology, bath-storage, calibration and behavior-integration gates. SI-core unit tests do not automatically validate the currently displayed prototype or make these sprites physically correct.

**Acceptance:** binary-alpha body/effects or another explicitly reviewed representation; matte/fringe cleanup on multiple backgrounds; consistent body/rim/water registration; feet at both actual bath supports; reviewed foreground masking and ground-Y order; restrained ripples/splashes tied to actual bathing contact; bounded resources/replay/reduced motion; separate visual and hydrology acceptance; no unsupported drinking/dry-state claim; provenance and immutable reversible versions.
