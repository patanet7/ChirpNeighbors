# Authoritative wood / foliage-only attachment pilot

This bounded pilot demonstrates a controllable way to place four generated foliage materials on existing wood. It does not resolve the old whole canopy. Its attachment status remains **HOLD**. No production variant or new taxon is submitted.

## Protocol

1. Inspect the frozen Engelmann oak wood at full resolution and enlarged central/right nodes. Preserve its original bytes, canvas and root placement. Select near-terminal twig nodes manually; retain initial guesses and subsequent refinement. The assumed ±6 source-pixel selection tolerance is not a measured confidence interval or identification of botanical buds.
2. Give image generation only the inspected project-generated isolated healthy-leaf style/shape candidate. Exclude the tree and previous canopy from generation inputs. Request one transparent 2×2 sheet of foliage with visible compact green bases; explicitly prohibit wood, bark, branches, brown stems and shoot axes. This is the material control that avoids a second competing scaffold. Exact prompt, one returned original and exposed settings are preserved. No species-photo pixels were supplied.
3. Losslessly extract four rectangular RGBA crops. Actual visible counts are A=4, B=3, C=3, D=3 leaves; B did not meet the requested four-to-five count. Retain it as a method material with this discrepancy, not a botanical acceptance. Visually inspect each base. Green petioles and pale veins remain authored foliage; no competing woody network is visible. This visual judgment is not a semantic segmentation proof or validation of natural oak leaf arrangement.
4. Attach A→T1, D→T2, B→T3, C→T4. All transforms are explicit nearest-neighbor scale/translation, no rotation, recolor, cleanup, painted connector or invented branch. Wood has the identity transform and remains a separate file. Source extraction equality is checked pixel-for-pixel.
5. Render three separate checks: ordinary physical resize, physical phase-locked sampling, and an enlarged diagnostic. All use the same wood node coordinates. The nearest-neighbor sampler and integer rounding are recorded explicitly; at very small dimensions x/y scale can differ slightly. Physical scale assumes a 5 cm representative leaf and inherits a provisional 9 m tree/1034 px visible height; it is a design test, not a measured specimen or calibrated botanical scale. Diagnostic cluster width is 64 px, with leaf axes about42–49 px, roughly7–9 times physical-check size. It is explicitly not a production scale.
6. Ordinary resize can omit a tiny base. Phase locking maps a chosen source base pixel center onto the target output pixel center through an explicit inverse affine nearest-neighbor sampler. This chooses existing source pixels; it does not paint or synthesize a connector. Preserve the ordinary failure, the sampling matrices and before/after triptychs. Reproduce with `python analysis/build_pilot.py` after extracting the package.

## Visual review and limits

Full source sheet, all four extracted bases, all four enlarged triptychs, physical sampling examples and light/dark tree previews were inspected. Enlarged bases meet their recorded nodes and no additional woody framework is visible. T1/T2 nearby groups can enter each other's inspection crop. Leaves obscure nearby wood in composite, particularly the enlarged central group; the underlying wood pixels remain present and unchanged. Parent scoped review inspected enlarged and phase-locked T1 triptychs and agreed that mechanical pinning is demonstrated, while biological attachment and full canopy remain unverified. Four attachments constitute a partial method demonstration, not a filled canopy or recovered hidden geometry. No per-leaf bud orientation, terminal status, petiole morphology, occlusion masks, full branch coverage or wind deformation has been independently validated.

Source art is more textured than strict small-grid pixel work in places. Strict pixel-style, species morphology, design scale, owner acceptance and production integration are pending. A successful control method is not approval of the material's botany or visual style.

State coverage is healthy leaf-on only. Separate drought stress, older/dry loss, wet-surface response, and seasonal/individual leaf-density changes are needs, not produced animation. Engelmann oak can be semi-/drought-deciduous with season and water variability; no universal orange autumn, evergreen guarantee or snow state is asserted. Prior text references remain in the preserved original intake; no new botanical fact validation is claimed in this method pass.

Wind metadata defines a fixed wood root and each foliage base attached to its selected wood node. A future rig would keep the petiole base constrained, deform distal foliage and propagate parent bough motion; no rig, animation, physics, emitter or runtime interaction is implemented. Potential shed-leaf emitters are the same node list with state gating pending. Cluster layers have no independent ground collision; the tree ground ellipse at[680,1157] with radii[245,30] is inherited provisionally.

## Next production gate

A full canopy would require an inspected wood node graph, explicit per-cluster source base and destination node, sampling-phase preservation at the approved output scale, front/back occlusion choices, and visual/node-by-node review at actual play scale. Density/state coverage must be authored separately; mechanically pinning four materials cannot infer hidden branches or validate the complete crown. No further generation or broad production rollout is part of this pilot.
