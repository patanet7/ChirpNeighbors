# Compact crow ground travel: evidence and test
2026-10-17. Owner direction: light upright walk-hop, short steps, small foot lifts and modest bounce; no deep crouch or large launch.

## Verified observations
American Crow, Corvus brachyrhynchos: Timothy Barksdale, April 2000, near Seeley Lake, Missoula, Montana. Macaulay Library ML416478, 85-second ground-foraging clip. https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/416478
Viewed in the cloud browser after normal page loading; sampled the first 8 seconds for frame-by-frame observation. The bird mixes short movement bursts with upright scanning pauses, wings folded. Feet remain much more closely associated with the torso than the earlier generated kicks. The camera pans and grass obscures toe contacts; this is qualitative observation, not calibrated kinematics. The small samples do not establish a clean complete airborne hop or exact footfall timing. Head and body orientation change while looking around, so an endlessly repeated identical march is also an inadequate behavioral model.

## Guide changes
The previous wide split and dramatic leg swap were control tests, now rejected as the natural-motion target. New original SVG guides cluster visible leg emergence under the belly (around x490/520 instead of x415/545 in 768-wide guide space), reduce foot separation to approximately 41 pixels, and reduce swing-foot lift to around 13 pixels. These are conservative proposed art bounds, not measurements of this species. No human OpenPose model or universal bird skeleton used.
A compact opposite-step guide and a provisional low-hop guide are included as drafts. The hop guide uses only a six-pixel body rise with feet nearby and no deep crouch; it has NOT been validated against a clean airborne reference or rendered by imagegen. It must not be described as motion-captured or biologically calibrated.
